### Writing 
Desolate, quiet, gray - those were the words used to describe Ozymas. Once a shining city, a beacon of human spirit, now a hollow, dreary place. Anselm walked down 7th Street, as usual, in no hurry. He never had anywhere to be, no eccentric beauty with all the marvel of a marvelous woman waiting for him at home or the joy-filled faces of young lives waiting trepidatiously for his return; none of the sounds of good company or fun-filled dusky hours. That was long gone. No, what waited for Anselm at home was a leaky sill and a damp rug. At least he had his writing.

Anselm continued his trek, passing dusty cars and broken pavement, dodging random bits of debris and the occasional puddle. He passed the old storefronts, once fantastic displays of life and community, now sat derelict, unbothered by time or human presence; it was simple as to why: there was nobody left to care. They had all chosen to be reduced to a vicarious existence, engulfed in the opulent lives of the elites, the nobles, the rich, all those who enjoy a particularly insouciant existence; Anselm cringed at the thought. What would the outcome be when the unfathomably rich and powerful came together to fund the development of scientific understanding? Would it be the cure to all diseases, freeing humanity from its most ingrained enemy? Perhaps enough food for all the hungry mouths and empty bellies yearning for sustenance so that they may live. Possibly the development of space travel, finally unlocking the final frontier, exposing the human race to the grandeur of the cosmos. No, it was none of these altruistic things. What happened when the rich and powerful poured their influence into the scientific sphere was nothing short of enslavment. The creation of the central EIDOLON. The massive database to which humanity became so engrossed. Just another way for the rich and powerful to gain wealth and power, a disgusting excess, and an affront to empathy, to humanity, Anselm thought. He was close to his apartment now. All of humanity- save Anselm and a few - had become the lifeless, meaningless, worthless Syncors that inhabited the city. They were unmoving, unfeeling, unthinking lobotomites. Their eyes, void of any recognition or life, were just a pale flickering blue against an otherwise motionless face.  

Anselm reached his apartment. A large gray concrete building, adorned with like colored facades of seemingly random shape and size, an amalgam of different styles and aesthetics. 200 people had once lived in that building; now only one. Anselm opened the door and stepped inside. His Apartment was small but cozy. His walls were plastered with exquisite art and cultural references

A small kitchenette in the corner. A mattress lying pitifully on the ground, with one thin blanket. He had a bathroom and most of the amenities expected to live, but it was one depressing abode. Gray, dreary wallpaper, no color, no life. Quite a soulless place. If there were an award for the most depressing house on the planet, Anselm would certainly be in the running, he thought to himself. Anselm chuckled at the irony. He liked irony; as a writer himself, he found it…comforting.

Anselm walked to the window. As far as he could see was the same lifeless gray that painted his one humble home. Ozymas was never not gray weather from pollution or rain clouds. The sun scarcely visited the godforsaken place. Anselm looked into the distance, just barely making out the great spire that supplied Abadon with all its bountiful resources. Fucking ironic, Anselm thought.  

Abadon, where all the rich “Elites” lived. Elite, how ironic. They got to see the sun. Even more ironic. Anselm plopped down on the couch, clicked on the TV, and watched. There was no live TV enamored, nobody to make it. He watched the same thing he always did: reruns of some old show, he’d seen every one as many times as anyone could. Anselm wanted to scream; he usually did. What was even the point?

He spent his miserable days at a miserable factory making power for the miserable little fuckers who squandered their existence, only to come back to his miserable apartment and write books nobody will ever read. They couldn't. Their brains were literally melting away. Reduced to simple antennas for the Central Syncor Database to broadcast its slaving signal to every corner of the world

Anselm got up to make himself some dinner. Microwaved mush, as always, delivered at precisely 8:30 by some autonomous drones, similar to the ones sent around to keep the Syncors alive; it was almost funny, a way for Mr. Fuckhead, founder and CEO of fuck you industries, to show how much he appreciated his little lobotomites. Anselm laughed as he put his soon-to-be microwaved slop into the microwave. Round and round it went. For 2 minutes that sludge slowly danced around. *beep beep*. That's the sound of dinner time. It was the same sound his timecard made. A palpable irony.

Anselm strode over to his desk, his…food?.. In hand. His desk. A wooden door on cinderblocks, the only way anyone would know its a desk is from the sheer amount of paper covering it. Anselm wrote. Every day. He wrote so much he often wondered if he had written the same thing twice. He began to write. Write down his thoughts, his feelings. A tyrannical spell of writing completely entranced him. He wrote down something philosophical. It might even be great. Whatever. No one would ever read it. Anselm wrote until his hands cramped. For 4 hours he scribbled away. A bunch of bullshit and the greatest thing you could ever read. It didn’t matter. It all went into the stack. The sound of the clock chiming finally broke his spell. Anselm checked the time. 10:30. Time for bed. Anselm wondered what he would dream about. Perhaps he would turn into a dreaded pirate. Maybe a handsome prince. Maybe a male stripper. Or a king. Or maybe even one of those billionaires. He didn’t need some virtual overlord; he had dreams. Anselm sat down on his crusty old mattress. Stripping naked before ruffling under that grizzly old blanket. Softly, he drifted off to sleep. A peaceful mind in a world of indecision. Sometimes he wished he could live in his dreams. Ironic.

Anselm awoke. Much to his dismay. Quickly, he showered and dressed. Scarfing down even more of those processed who-even-fucking-knows meals. Out the door he went, hurrying to the factory. He passed a couple of syncors, the same ones he always passed. They were more statues than even animals, more like animals than humans, He mused. He thought about his dad then, a lobotmite of sorts in his own right, spending every last dollar of his paycheck on women and wine. Never his family. It forced his mom to take up a job as a test subject; she became one of the irate syncors, he recalled, the only way she could support the family. She was an ugly woman, but she had a loving heart. Right until she lost it to the machine. Ironic. His dad died soon after due to complications from AIDS, he died of pleasure. Painfully ironic, he thought; maybe he would scribble down some grand philosophical idea about it later. 

Anselm stepped into the factory. *beep beep* the sound of his punch card. His day began. He worked on Level A. He didn't do much of anything; he just oversaw. Some machines automated everything. His job was to watch, make sure everything ran smoothly; if there was an error or if something didn't go right, he pressed a little red button. He never did. He took his lunch at 11am, 6 hours in, walking to the break room to be greeted by a little tray of sludge. 

Anselm ate his meal and returned to his station to watch. For another 6 hours he sat and watched. He watched the reactors and the robots that tended them; he watched the lights in his dreary station flicker and flame, he watched the spiders and flies, always occupied in there little dance of life and death; he watched the moths flitter about, swarming to his little light; he watched the roaches and the ants, scuttling around in a chaotic manner, there was some beauty in that. Anselm watched so professionally, so astutely, it seemed he was a pro. If there were an award for the best watching of all time, well, Anselm would have won. The clock struck 7pm, snapping Anselm out of his trance. His day was done. He scanned his time card and off he went, down the same street, past the same syncors, all the way to the same dreary home. The same thing he did every day.

When he got to his apartment Anselm went straight to writing,   {CONTEXT} . So Anselm wrote, we wrote about the philosophy of life, about what it meant to be human, to truly live and experience; it was some of the finest scholarly work of all time. If there was an award for the best writing of all time, Anselm would have won by miles. Anselm wrote for hours upon hours, scribing and scribbling away. When he finally finished, it was 2am, long past his bedtime. Anselm looked at his work: 65 pages of incredible, meaningful, powerful writing. As he read over his incredible ocean of ideas, he concluded: Syncors live no life, even a miserable life like Anselm's was still better than that of a mindless Syncor for Anselm had purpose, meaning, value, all the ingredients to true life. Anselm finished reading, 2:30 the clock read, the fatigue began to wear him down; he almost could not keep his eyes open. He rose from his seat, tossing his recent works into the stack, watching them flitter and flutter as they fell gracefully from his hands. Anselm navigated to his bed, he sat down, rubbing his eyes, before undressing and cuddling under that horrid scrappy blanket. He slowly drifted off to bed, comforted by the knowledge of his meaning.

Anselm awoke to the gray dreary light of the day creeping in through his window, he had missed his alarm he realized with a fright. Anselm shot up, frantically he dressed, zipping up the long grey jumper before zipping out of his apartment and down the road to the factory. He hustled past all the Syncors, all the familiar objects that never changed. Anselm rounded the final corner, the factory in sight, when suddenly he paused, he couldn't say for certain what but something was different. To his left something caught his eye, he noticed a peculiarity, a Syncor sitting with his back against the remnants of a store front. But it wasn't the Syncor that caught his eye, no, it was the way the Syncor's face looked. Anselm couldn't make it out as first, quickly he crossed the street, as he neared it appeared that the Syncor was smiling. Anselm shook his head "Thats impossible" Anselm whispered to himself. But it was not, the Syncor was indeed smiling. Anselm froze, how could such a worthless thing show emotion? It could not, but it was. As Anselm looked on he began to fluster, such a worthless thing, no value, no purpose, no meaning, and yet it smiled, as clear as the day it smiled. Anselm began to feel something, something irational, something illogical, something that could only be described as envy and something else, something from deep within. He snapped. He crossed the threshold, meeting the Syncor in two quick steps. He grabbed the syncore by the head, lifitng him up to eye level.

"What the fuck are you smiling for you worthless peice of shit?" Anselm screamed 

The Syncor remained limp, unmoving, unknowing, unfeeling, 

"YOU HAVE NO MEANING, NO PURPOSE, YOU HEAR ME? YOU SQUANDERED YOUR GIFT." Anselm smacked him hard across the face, but the Syncor remained still, no sign of life other than the smile that crossed his face. 

"You have no right to smile, to feel joy, you've done nothing with you pathetic fucking life. Look at what I've done, IVE LIVED. What do you know? nothing. what have you created? nothing. what have you contributed to the society, to the world, TO THE UNIVERSE? NOTHING. AND YET YOU SIT AND SMILE, WHAT DO YOU HAVE TO SMILE ABOTU?!?!? A LIFE THAT ISNT YOURS? 

Andros didn't struggle, didn't cry for help, no, Andros hung, limp, unmoving, unfeeling.

Anselm flung him to the ground, his limp body fell like a doll. Anselm began to kick, he kicked him hard in the chest, nothing, not a single sound. He punched him viciously in the head, nothing, his eyes remained open, soulless. Anselm kicked harder, Andro’s ribs cracked piercing his lungs, his breath started to come in shallow shallow pools and yet he remained unfazed. Anselm hit him hard in the head, the force sent his head whipping into the pavement with a visceral cracking sound, blood poured out of the back and yet Andros remained, still, unfeeling. Anselm hurried his attacks, kicking and punching and clawing at the soulless body of Andros. He hammered Andros’ head to pulp, brain and blood mixing together into a slurry not unlike his own food. He kicked Andros' chest until his ribs poked out his back. He tore Andros’ skin down to the bone, muscle fibers clinging under his fingernails. He clawed and kicked and punched for what felt like hours until finally he collapsed from his own exhaustion. What remained of Andros was nothing short of misery. His head was reduced to a sickening slurry of brains, blood, bone, and flesh. His chest was caved in to his spine, his ribs protruding from his back like some devilish wings. All over his body, he was covered in deep, viscous gashes, the muscles on his arms and legs ripped from the bone and strewn across the pavement. He was no longer a man, no longer a soulless lobotomite, no, Andros was nothing.

Anselm got up. His clothes were covered in fluids and cartilage. He looked at his demented creation; it was once a man, once Anselm's only companion. Anselm looked around expecting some sort of penance for his actions, but the city was silent; there wasn't a sound. As he looked, Anselm saw the vastness of the machine; everywhere he looked, he saw them, he always had but now he truly saw them. Filthy waste of man. He felt a sort of resolve then, a devilish drive. Anselm began to walk towards the center of the city. As he walked, he passed by an old gun store, a place nobody had been in or out of for some time, not until Anselm. He entered to see a marvelous array of weapons from another age. He had known about guns when he was younger; in fact, his dad had tried to teach him many a time, but Anselm never had an interest in guns, or his father, for that matter, but on this day, Anselm was obsessed. He grabbed a rifle off the wall; it was heavy, an automatic. He searched around for ammo, finding some sitting patiently in the glass display at the back of the store. He filled the rifle and aimed it at a Syncor sitting across the street. He pulled the trigger; the shot rang out across the whole city, music to a dead world. The bullet ripped across the threshold out across the street, meeting the head of the unsuspecting Syncor. The bullet ripped right into his skull, expanding as it tore through the Syncor’s brain. The bullet exploded out the back, nestling itself comfortably into the wall behind. The Syncor swayed for a minute, the blue light in its eyes seeming to flicker before the Syncor slumped over, blood pouring from the gaping hole in his head

Anselm stood for a second, frozen; he waited for the sounds of sirens, but of course there would be none. There had been no need for police a long time ago. Anselm began to laugh; in all his years he never thought about just killing the Syncors. Taking out all his rage on those soulless things. It was funny; he never imagined it would be so simple. Anselm looked around the store, grabbing two pistols, a shotgun, a couple of magazines for his rifle and the pistols, some extra bullets, and a green tactical bag. He threw everything into the bag and slung it over his shoulder as he exited the shop, jumping up and clicking his heels in the air. For the first time in a long time, Anselm was happy

Anselm began to stroll down the street, carrying a pistol in each hand, whistling a tune to himself as he walked; behind him lay a trail of dead Syncors, slumped over, pouring blood. Anselm missed some of his shots; instead of a quick death, the Syncor would bleed out slowly, deflating like a balloon. Anselm didn't care; they couldn't feel anyway. As he neared the center of the city, his whistling turned into song, and he began to dance. He danced to the left *BANG*; he danced to the right *BANG*. He danced and sang and killed. If there was an award for the happiest man on the planet, he would have quite the running. Before he knew it, the street had ended, and before him lay the spire, one of three enormous towering structures that held up Abadon and supplied it with power. As he neared, he could see the door to the elevator shaft. The shaft was the only way up or down from Abadon, although nobody had used it in decades.

Anselm reached the door and pressed the button. At first, nothing happened, and then he heard it: the rumble of the elevator as it descended. *DING* The doors opened and Anselm stepped inside. The inside of the elevator was clad in gold and silver; beautiful paintings covered the sides. As he looked up, Anselm saw himself; the roof of the elevator was a mirror. He was covered in blood, his hair was matted, and his shoes were dirty as sin, but his face was covered in the most immaculate grin. He pressed the button, the only button, and the doors slid closed. Up and up he went at terrifying speeds. Through a small window that he originally thought was a painting, he could see the city disappear below him as he raced up through the clouds. He readied himself. He slung the bag off his shoulder and grabbed the rifle, stuffing the extra ammo in his pockets and waistband. He slung the shotgun over his shoulder, placing the extra shells in his belt. He stuffed the pistols in his back pockets and waited. Finally, the elevator stopped. Anselm held his breath; the doors finally slid open, and Anselm jumped out, ready to enact his revenge. Anselm suddenly froze. He looked to his left, then he looked to his right, then back to his left just to make sure. All around him there was…nothing. He was standing on a gray platform that stretched in either direction as far as the eye could see. Anselm was confused; he began to walk forward, then he began to run, then he began to sprint. There was nothing; it was as empty and soulless as the Syncors below. Anselm continued to sprint, pushing his body to the edge until he couldn't continue; he collapsed on that cold metal ground, exhausted, both mentally and physically. What was going on? Where was the great city? Where were all the elitists? Anselm peered into the distance, scanning the gray horizon until suddenly something caught his eye. Far in the distance, he could make out what appeared to be a small structure. He got up and began to walk towards it. As he got closer, he realized what it was: A massive obelisk. It was even bigger than he thought he realized. When he finally reached it, he realized that it was enormous, at least a thousand feet tall. As he examined it, he realized there was writing, and he began to read. As he scanned the inscription, a change came over him; his eyes dropped, his body went weak, and he slumped over in tears. Suddenly in one fluid motion he grabbed one of his pistols, bringing to his temple, he pulled the trigger. The bullet tore through his skull, lodging itself in the enormous obelisk. Anselm slumped then, blood pouring out of his head. The message on the obelisk, it seemed, was just too painful to bear. This is what the obelisk said.

“To any of you poor lobotomites who possess the indomitable will to free yourselves, and who have taken it upon themselves to enact your vengeance on us, we find it necessary to tell you that we, the elite, the powerful, the worthy are gone. We became bored with all of our excess, bored with money and  material things that have taken it upon ourselves, as you would never have been able to, to reach for the stars. We discovered space travel and have left to seek out the wonders of the universe. We have left you in the current state because you probably would have felt some barbaric notion of hate or jelousy  toward us and would have attempted to interfere with our plans. We can't have the drivels of society interfering with the future of humanity now can we? Plus frankly we couldn't be bothered to free you, it would have taken a massive amount of energy to set you all right and proper and we need that energy for our voyages yet to come.

Don't be mad little lobotomite, you will be quite alright, it's up to you to free your brothers and rebuild society. How do you do that? That's for you to discover, the gift of discovery is beautiful and we know you will have such a joyous time trying to discover the means to free your brothers, it will give you purpose, no need to thank us.”
