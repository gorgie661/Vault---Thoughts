Your perception is inherently a subjective factor of your understanding. What you perceive is entirely based upon the current state of your mind. We perceive what are brains decide we perceive. we have certain filters and systems to block out certain information. The question therefore is can we definitively say that a specific mode of an individuals perception is the basic perception? Or on other words, can we determine without a doubt what normal perception is for for anyone? 

Perception is the process of organizing, interpreting, and understanding sensory information to represent and make sense of the environment around us. We can generalize this to perception is the process of interpreting information about our environment.

It is a well known fact that our brains posses certain filters that remove certain information we collect from our 5 senses. This raises a series of questions: what is life truly like? What is our environment truly like? and most importantly; How do you definitively know what the correct perception is? It can be argued that  correct perception can be generalized to either of the following; what is most normally perceived and secondly, what the majority most generally perceives. For example, if your car has always been blue and suddenly you perceive it as red, you can make a logical claim that you are hallucinating and that what you are seeing is in fact wrong, moreover, few will argue that a red stop sign is green.  However both these arguments are fallacious, one relies on the sentiment of the majority and the other is an appeal to tradition. You cannot say something is definitively something just because it has always been. Lets say you were to take acid and look at your car. it was blue when you were sober but now its red. Is the car definitively red or blue? you could say its blue because that's the color it is when you are sober but are you not still perceiving when high? Can we say that being intoxicated is definitively a false perception? further still you cannot say something is definitely something simply because the majority says so, a colorblind person could see the same stop sign and perceive it as a complexly different color. You cant say his perception is anymore wrong than yours is without falling to a contradistinction or fallacy.  

If we return to the car example, it is well known that acid and other drugs alter your perception. but is that an appropriate thing to say? an altered perception? or is it brand new perception?
Lets look back at the example again. Lets say you decide to take acid. your walls start moving, your car turns a different color. Are those perceptions false? 'Normal' perception is inherently false, your brain filters out information. we could just as easily argue that drugs are suppressed perception just as easily as we can argue they are heightened. Drugs alter the informational filters in your brain allowing different information in so is that perception wrong? is it even altered? No, it is complexly new. When on drugs you are granted an entirely new perception, your brain is filtering different information and thus you are experiencing and perceiving complete new things. that doesn't make them any less wrong, just new. 

you cannot definitively say that any perception is correct or wrong with out being exposed to contradiction or a fallacious argument. the simple fact is that perception is inherently subjective to your brain chemistry, when you alter your brain chemistry you are exposed to new information and thus new perception. we can prove this further still with an interesting thought experiment., if you were to take a child from the womb and immediately hook them up to acid to the point where they are constantly high, the child would adapt and function perfectly fine and logically within the confines of their perceived environment. Essentially, that child will be react and function perfectly normally in regards to their perception. If that child were to be taken off acid their perception would resemble ours but for them this new perception is wrong. Neither mode of perception is right or wrong because there simply isn't an answer to right or wrong perception, it is entirely subjective
